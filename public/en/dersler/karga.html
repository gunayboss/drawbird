How to draw a hooded crow

Easy

The crow you see most often in Turkey has a gray body and a black head. That is the one we draw in this lesson. A thick beak and wingtips that spread like fingers are the secret.

The dashed line is the wing line in the DrawBird canvas: everything above it flaps, everything below it stays as the body.

Step by step

1. Gray body

Draw a big, long egg below the dashed line and color it gray. A crow is much bigger than a sparrow, so keep the body wide.

2. Black head

Put a round black head on the right of the body. Add a small white shine on the eye, or the eye gets lost in the black.

3. Thick beak

In front of the head, draw a thick, strong black beak that curves slightly down. The crow eats everything with this beak: seeds, insects, walnuts.

4. Wing with fingers

ABOVE the dashed line, draw a wide black wing. Do not end the tip flat: make feathers that split apart like fingers. You can recognize a flying crow from far away by these fingers.

5. Round tail

Add a black tail on the left of the body, rounded at the end like a fan.

Done!

Hooded crow is ready. Now draw it in DrawBird, give it a name and let it loose in the sky.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the beak thin. A crow's beak is thick and strong.
  • Making everything black. This crow's body is gray. Only the head, wings and tail are black.
  • Ending the wingtip in a point. The separated finger feathers are what make a crow a crow.

What you should know about Hooded crow

  • It is 44–51 cm long, with a wingspan of 84–100 cm.
  • It breaks hard-shelled food like walnuts or mussels by flying up high and dropping them on the ground.
  • It can hide extra food and find the spot again later.
  • It is very close to its all-black relative in western Europe. For many years the two were counted as the same species.
