How to draw a barn swallow

Easy

One thing is enough to recognize a swallow: the forked tail. The rest is a small body and pointed wings. It takes five steps.

The dashed line is the wing line in the DrawBird canvas: everything above it flaps, everything below it stays as the body.

Step by step

1. Body first

Under the dashed line, draw a small, slim body and color it navy. A swallow isn't plump. Think of a pencil stretching forward.

2. Head and beak

Add a small head on the right of the body. Make the beak a tiny triangle. A swallow catches insects in the air with its mouth open, so it doesn't need a big beak.

3. Pointed wing

ABOVE the dashed line, draw a long, thin wing with a pointed tip. Curve it back a little. If you draw a round wing, your bird starts to look like a sparrow.

4. Forked tail

On the left of the body, draw a tail with two pointed tips. Make the outer tips long and let the middle dip inward. This is the swallow's signature.

5. Colors

Put a brick-red patch on the throat and a cream stripe under the belly. With these two colors, the navy back shines even more.

Done!

Barn swallow is ready. Now draw it in DrawBird, give it a name and let it loose in the sky.

Common mistakes

  • Ending the tail flat. Without the fork, it's not a swallow.
  • Drawing a plump body. A swallow is a slim, light bird.
  • Making the wing short and round. The wing should be long and pointed.

What you should know about Barn swallow

  • The barn swallow is 17–19 cm with its tail streamers and weighs 16–22 g.
  • It catches its food in the air. It also drinks by flying low over the water.
  • It builds its nest from hundreds of mud pellets that it carries in its beak.
  • It spends the winter in Africa and often comes back to the place where it was born in spring.
