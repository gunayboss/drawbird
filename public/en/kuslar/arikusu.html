European bee-eater

Merops apiaster

Turquoise, yellow and chestnut birds that line up on wires in summer calling "prrrip prrrip". One of Europe's most colorful birds is right here with us.

Size25–29 cm (with tail streamers)
Wingspan36–40 cm
Weight45–70 g
DietBees, wasps, dragonflies and flying insects
Eggs4–7
Where to see itOpen land, riverbanks; April–September

The back is chestnut and yellow, the throat bright yellow, the chest turquoise. The two feathers in the middle of the tail are longer than the others.

Its name isn't for nothing, it mainly eats bees. What about the sting? After catching a bee, it takes it to a branch and hits and rubs it on the branch until the sting comes out and the venom is gone. Then it swallows it happily.

It builds its nest in earth banks and sand walls on riverbanks. It digs tunnels longer than a meter with its beak. Always in colonies, side by side.

They spend the winter in Africa. Some beekeepers complain about them, but research says a colony generally doesn't do serious harm to an apiary.

To draw it in DrawBird

Use the colorful bird idea from the parrot lesson, but make the beak long, thin and slightly curved. Add two long feathers in the middle of the tail. Throat yellow, chest turquoise, back chestnut.
