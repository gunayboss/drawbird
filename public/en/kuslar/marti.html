Yellow-legged gull

Larus michahellis

The gull that follows the ferry and tries to snatch your simit is most likely a yellow-legged gull.

Size52–68 cm
Wingspan120–155 cm
Weight750–1550 g
DietFish, sea creatures, leftover food
Eggs2–3
Where to see itAll coasts, big lakes, cities; all year

An adult gull has a light gray back and black-and-white spotted wing tips. It gets its name from its yellow legs. It also has a red dot on its lower beak, and I'll get to that in a moment.

Gulls are much smarter than you think. They drop mussels onto rocks from above to break them. They learn when people eat. Some of them wait at the pier as if they knew the ferry schedule.

That red dot is a button for the chicks. When the chick pecks the dot, the mother or father brings up food and gives it.

If you see a brown speckled gull, don't think it's another species. It's a young gull. It can take four years to get its adult feathers.

They can drink sea water too. Glands above their eyes get rid of the extra salt. The water dripping from the tip of their beaks is that salty liquid.

To draw it in DrawBird

Follow the gull lesson. Make the wings long and light gray, and paint the tips black. Let the legs be yellow and the dot on the beak red.
