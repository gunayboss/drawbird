Barn swallow

Hirundo rustica

A summer guest with a forked tail that builds mud nests under the eaves of barns and porches. The barn swallow.

Size17–19 cm (with tail streamers)
Wingspan32–35 cm
Weight16–22 g
DietFlying insects
Eggs4–5
Where to see itVillages, farms, waterside; March–October

The back is shiny navy blue, the belly cream, and the forehead and throat brick red. The two outer tail feathers are long and thin. Males have longer tails, and females choose the long-tailed males.

A swallow catches its food in the air. It drinks in the air too, flying very low over the water. Before rain, insects fly lower, so swallows fly lower too. That's where the saying "if swallows fly low, it will rain" comes from.

It builds its nest from hundreds of mud pellets it carries in its beak. The same pair returns to the same nest for years and repairs it.

They spend the winter in Africa. Every year they travel thousands of kilometers there and back, and often return in spring to the village where they were born. They really do know the address.

To draw it in DrawBird

Use the long wing idea from the gull lesson, but draw the bird small and slim. Make the tail a fork with two pointed tips. Back navy, throat red, belly white.
